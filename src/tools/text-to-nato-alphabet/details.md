# Text to NATO Phonetic Alphabet

## What it does
This tool spells out any text using the NATO phonetic alphabet, replacing each letter with its code word: A becomes Alpha, B becomes Bravo, C becomes Charlie, and so on. It is a communication aid, useful whenever a word must be spelled out clearly over a noisy or low-quality channel.

## Why someone uses it
- Reading out a reference number, licence key, email address or booking code over the phone without confusion between letters like B/D/P or M/N.
- Learning the alphabet used in aviation, maritime, emergency and amateur-radio communication.
- Preparing scripts or training materials for call-centre and support staff.
- Writing clear spelled-out instructions for a colleague.

## Step-by-step instructions
1. Type or paste your text into the input field.
2. The NATO spelling appears in the card below, one code word per letter.
3. Click *Copy NATO string* to copy the result.

## Examples
- `SOS` → Sierra Oscar Sierra
- `Cab` → Charlie Alpha Bravo
- `A7X` → Alpha 7 Xray (digits and other characters that are not letters are passed through unchanged)

## Understanding the results
Each letter is replaced by a word chosen so that the words sound distinct even with static or accents: "Bravo" and "Delta" are hard to confuse, unlike "B" and "D". The output is separated by single spaces. Letters are case-insensitive, so "a" and "A" both produce Alpha. Characters that are not letters, such as digits, spaces and punctuation, are kept as they are. The tool covers the standard 26 letters; it does not reproduce the special spoken forms for digits used in some radio procedures (for example "Niner" for 9).

## FAQs
- **Is this an official military tool?** No. The NATO phonetic alphabet is widely used by civilians in aviation, telecoms and customer service. Armytool is an independent site with no connection to any military or government body.
- **Is the alphabet exactly the same in every organisation?** The core 26 words are standardised by the International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO) and adopted by NATO, but some organisations use local alternatives.
- **Can it decode phonetic words back to text?** Not currently; it works from text to phonetic words.

## Common mistakes
- Expecting accented letters (é, ñ) to be converted; they pass through unchanged.
- Treating "Alfa" and "Alpha" as different: both spellings are seen, the official ICAO spelling being "Alfa" and the common English spelling "Alpha".
- Spelling out an entire sentence when only the critical characters need clarification.
